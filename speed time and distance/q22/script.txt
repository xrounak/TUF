Now in this problem, we are given that the speed of the bus without stoppages is 60 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

But including stoppages, its average speed becomes 45 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The question is asking us to find how many minutes the bus stops in every one hour.

Now let us think about it in a very simple way.

Suppose the bus keeps moving continuously for 1 hour without any stop.

At a speed of 60 kilo-mee-ter per hour, it will cover 60 kilo-mee-ter.

Correct?

Now look at the second condition.

Including stoppages, the average speed is only 45 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

That means in 1 hour of "clock time", the bus covers only 45 kilo-mee-ter.

Now compare these two distances.

Without stopping, it could have covered 60 kilo-mee-ter.

But because of stoppages, it covered only 45 kilo-mee-ter.

So the bus "missed" covering 15 kilo-mee-ter.

Got it? Perfect.

Now ask yourself one question.

If the bus is moving at 60 kilo-mee-ter per hour, how much time does it take to cover 15 kilo-mee-ter?

We know,

Time is equal to Distance divided by Speed.

So,

Time is equal to 15 divided by 60 hour.

After simplifying this,

we get 1 by 4 hour.

And 1 by 4 hour is equal to 15 minutes.

So this 15 minutes is the time for which the bus was standing still in every one hour.

Hence, the bus stops for "15 minutes per hour."

So the correct answer is "Option B."
