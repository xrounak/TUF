Now in this problem, we are given that the downstream speed of a boat is 24 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The upstream speed of the same boat is 16 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The question is asking us to find the speed of the current.

Now first, let us assume,

The speed of the boat in still water is B kilo-mee-ter per hour.

And the speed of the current is C kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Now let us understand what "downstream" and "upstream" mean.

Imagine that the river is flowing in this direction.

The boat itself can move at B kilo-mee-ter per hour in still water.

The river is also flowing at C kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Now when the boat moves in the "same direction" as the river, both the boat and the current help each other.

So their speeds get added.

That is why,

Downstream Speed is equal to Speed of the Boat in Still Water pluss Speed of the Current.

Or simply,

B pluss C.

Now let us look at the opposite case.

When the boat moves "against" the flow of the river, the current tries to slow the boat down.

So the speed of the current gets subtracted from the speed of the boat.

That is why,

Upstream Speed is equal to Speed of the Boat in Still Water my-nus Speed of the Current.

Or simply,

B my-nus C.

Now let us use the given values.

The downstream speed is 24.

So,

B pluss C is equal to 24.

The upstream speed is 16.

So,

B my-nus C is equal to 16.

Now we need to find the speed of the current, which is C.

So let us subtract the second equation from the first.

On the left side,

B pluss C my-nus B my-nus C.

The B terms cancel each other.

So we are left with 2 C.

On the right side,

24 my-nus 16, which is 8.

So,

2 C is equal to 8.

Dividing both sides by 2,

we get C is equal to 4.

So the speed of the current is "4 kilo-mee-ter per hour."

Hence, the correct answer is "Option D."
