Now in this problem, we are given that the downstream speed of a boat is 30 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Also, the speed of the current is 5 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The question is asking us to find the "upstream speed" of the boat.

Now before solving, let us understand one simple concept.

Imagine that the river is flowing in one direction.

When the boat moves in the "same direction" as the river, the current helps the boat move faster.

So both the speeds get added.

That is why,

Downstream Speed is equal to Speed of Boat in Still Water pluss Speed of Current.

Now let the speed of the boat in still water be B kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The downstream speed is already given as 30.

And the speed of the current is 5.

So,

B pluss 5 is equal to 30.

After solving this,

B is equal to 25 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Now this value will help us find the upstream speed.

Why? Because upstream means the boat is moving "against" the flow of the river.

So this time, the current slows the boat down.

That is why,

Upstream Speed is equal to Speed of Boat in Still Water my-nus Speed of Current.

Now we already know the speed of the boat in still water.

It is 25 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

So,

Upstream Speed is equal to 25 minus 5.

After simplifying this,

we get 20 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

So the upstream speed of the boat is "20 kilo-mee-ter per hour."

Hence, the correct answer is "Option B."
