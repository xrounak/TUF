Now in this problem, we are given that one cyclist is 24 kilo-mee-ter behind another cyclist.

The speed of the faster cyclist is 54 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The speed of the slower cyclist is 42 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The question is asking us to find how long the faster cyclist will take to overtake the slower cyclist.

Now let us understand what is actually happening.

In one hour, the slower cyclist moves 42 kilo-mee-ter.

At the same time, the faster cyclist moves 54 kilo-mee-ter.

The faster cyclist covers the "extra" distance, which is 54 minus 42.

That is 12 kilo-mee-ter.

So in one hour, the gap reduces by only 12 kilo-mee-ter.

Got it? Perfect.

Now the total gap between them is 24 kilo-mee-ter.

If 12 kilo-mee-ter of the gap is reduced in 1 hour,

then 1 kilo-mee-ter of the gap will be reduced in 1 divided by 12 hour.

Therefore, to reduce a gap of 24 kilo-mee-ter,

the time taken will be 24 into 1 by 12.

That is equal to 24 divided by 12.

This gives us 2 hours.

Now let me tell you the formula for these types of questions.

Whenever two objects are moving in the "same direction",

always use the "difference of their speeds."

This is called the "relative speed."

So,

Relative Speed = 54 minus 42 = 12 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The distance to be covered is 24 kilo-mee-ter.

Now simply use the formula,

Time = Distance divided by Relative Speed.

Time = 24 divided by 12 = 2 hours.

So the faster cyclist will overtake the slower cyclist after "2 hours."

Hence, the correct answer is "Option B."
