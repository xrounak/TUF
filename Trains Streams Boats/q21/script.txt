Now in this problem, we are given the time taken by a launch to travel downstream and upstream.

The question is asking us to find the speed of the launch in "still water".

<break time="0.5s" />

Before solving, let us first understand these terms.

"Still water" means the water is not flowing at all.
The speed of the launch in still water is its own speed.

Let us take this speed as **b**.

Now rivers always have a flow.
The speed of this flowing water is called the "current" or "stream speed".

Let us take it as **c**.

Now what happens when the launch moves with the flow of the river?

The river pushes the launch forward.
So the launch becomes faster.

This is called **downstream**.

So,

Downstream Speed = **b + c**

<break time="0.5s" />

Now what if the launch moves against the flow?

The river tries to slow it down.

This is called **upstream**.

So,

Upstream Speed = **b − c**

Now this part is clear, and we can solve the question very easily.

The launch covers 30 kilo-mee-ter downstream in 2 hours.

So its downstream speed is

30 ÷ 2 = 15 km/h.

Similarly, it covers 30 kilo-mee-ter upstream in 3 hours.

So its upstream speed is

30 ÷ 3 = 10 km/h.

Now we already know,

Downstream Speed = b + c which is 15 and

Upstream Speed = b − c which is 10

Adding both,

b + c minus b - c = 15 + 10

here c gets cancel from both side so

2b = 25

After solving this,

b = 12.5 km/h.

<break time="0.6s" />

Hence, the speed of the launch in still water is **12.5 km/h**.

So the correct option is **Option A.**
