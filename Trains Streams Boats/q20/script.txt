Now in this problem, we are given that a train is 240 mee-ter long.

It passes a man, who is walking at 6 kilo-mee-ter per hour, in the opposite direction.

The train takes 12 seconds to completely pass the man.

The question is asking us to find the speed of the train.

Now the important words here are "opposite direction."
<break time="0.5s" />

What does this actually mean?

It means the train and the man are moving towards each other.

So, their speeds do not cancel.

Instead, they get added.

This is the basic rule to remember.

When two objects move in opposite directions,

Relative Speed = Sum of their speeds.

Now before using this rule, we first need the relative speed.

Why?

Because we already know the train length and the time taken to pass the man.

When a train passes a person, the distance covered is only the length of the train.

So,

Relative Speed = Distance divided by Time

= 240 divided by 12

= 20 mee-ter per second.

Done. Now we know the relative speed.

The man's speed is given in kilo-mee-ter per hour, so let us convert it into mee-ter per second.

6 multiplied by 5 divided by 18 gives us 5 by 3 mee-ter per second.

Now we can use the relation,

Relative Speed = Train Speed pluss Man's Speed.

So,

20 = Train Speed pluss 5 by 3.

After simplifying this, we get,

Train Speed = 55 by 3 mee-ter per second.

Now only one step is left.

The options are in kilo-mee-ter per hour, so we convert the speed back.

55 by 3 multiplied by 18 divided by 5 gives us 66 kilo-mee-ter per hour.
