Now in this problem, we are given that the speed of the boat in still water is 10 kilo-mee-ter per hour, and the speed of the stream is 2 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The question is asking us to find the "downstream speed."

Now the important word here is "downstream."
<break time="0.5s" />

What does downstream actually mean?

It means the boat is moving in the same direction as the flowing water.

Since both the boat and the stream are moving in the same direction, their speeds help each other.

So, for downstream movement, we always "add" the two speeds.

This is a simple rule to remember.

Downstream Speed = Boat Speed in Still Water + Stream Speed.

Got it? Perfect.

Now let us put the given values.

Boat speed = 10 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Stream speed = 2 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

So,

Downstream Speed = 10 pluss 2.

After adding them, we get 12 kilo-mee-ter per hour.
<break time="0.6s" />

Hence, the downstream speed of the boat is "12 kilo-mee-ter per hour."
