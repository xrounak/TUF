Now in this problem, we are given that the downstream speed of a boat is 16 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Also, the upstream speed is 10 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

The question is asking us to find the speed of the boat in still water.

Now let the speed of the boat in still water be B kilo-mee-ter per hour.

And let the speed of the current be C kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Now before putting the values, let us understand the formulas.

When the boat moves "with" the flow of the river, the current helps the boat.

So, the downstream speed is equal to the speed of the boat in still water plus the speed of the current.

That means, the downstream speed is equal to B plus C.

Similarly, when the boat moves "against" the flow of the river, the current slows the boat down.

So, the upstream speed is equal to the speed of the boat in still water minus the speed of the current.

That means, the upstream speed is equal to B minus C.

Got it? Perfect.

Now the downstream speed is given as 16.

So, B plus C becomes 16.

The upstream speed is given as 10.

So, B minus C becomes 10.

Now let us add these two equations.

Why are we adding them?

Because plus C and minus C cancel each other.

So only two times B is left.

On the other side, we get 16 plus 10, which is 26.

So, two times B is equal to 26.

Dividing both sides by 2, we get B equal to 13.

Since B represents the speed of the boat in still water,

the required speed is "13 kilo-mee-ter per hour."

Hence, the correct answer is "Option B."
