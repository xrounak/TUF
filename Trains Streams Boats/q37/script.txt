Now in this problem, the boat's still-water speed is 10 km/h.

The stream speed is 2 km/h.

And the distance for both upstream and downstream journeys is 24 kilo-mee-ter.

The question is asking us to find how much more time the upstream journey takes than the downstream journey.

Let us first recall the basic formulas.

When the boat moves "against" the stream, the stream slows it down.

So,

Upstream Speed = Boat Speed my-nus Stream Speed = 10 - 2 = 8 km/h.

When the boat moves "with" the stream, the stream helps it move faster.

So,

Downstream Speed = Boat Speed pluss Stream Speed = 10 + 2 = 12 km/h.

Done.

Now we know both speeds, so finding the time becomes easy.

We use the formula,

Time = Distance divided by Speed.

Upstream Time = 24 divided by 8 = 3 hours.

And,

Downstream Time = 24 divided by 12 = 2 hours.

Now the question is not asking for the individual times.

It is asking how much "more" time the upstream journey takes.

So,

Difference = 3 - 2 = 1 hour.
