Now let us first read the question.

A 210 mee-ter train passes a cyclist moving in the same direction at 6 kilo-mee-ter per hour in 14 seconds. We need to find the speed of the train.

Given,

Length of train = 210 mee-ter

Cyclist Speed = 6 kilo-mee-ter per hour

Time = 14 seconds

Let the speed of the train be "T" kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Now what is our approach?

The important words are "same direction".

When two objects move in the same direction, we never use the actual speed. We use "relative speed" because the train is only catching up with the cyclist.

So,

Relative Speed = Train Speed − Cyclist Speed

= T − 6.

Now we need the value of this relative speed.

When a train passes a cyclist, the cyclist is treated like a point.

So the train covers only its own length.

Hence,

Relative Speed = Distance ÷ Time

where distance covered is length of the train

so Relative speed = 210 ÷ 14

= 15 mee-ter per second.

But our variable T is in kilo-mee-ter per hour.

So first convert the relative speed.

15 × 3.6 = 54 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Now substitute this into our equation.

relative speed = T - 6 so 

T − 6 = 54.

Adding 6 on both sides,

T = 60 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

<break time="0.6s" />

Hence, the speed of the train is "60 kilo-mee-ter per hour".

So the correct option is "Option B".
