Now in this problem, a jogger runs at 9 kilo-mee-ter per hour instead of 6 kilo-mee-ter per hour, and because of the higher speed, she covers 18 kilo-mee-ter more.

The question is asking us to find the "actual distance" covered by the jogger.

Now, one thing is very important.

The time for which the jogger runs remains the same in both cases.
Only the speed changes.
So, let us take the running time as "t" hours.

Also, let the actual distance covered be "x" kilo-mee-ter.

Now let us write the given values.

The speed in the first case is 6 kilo-mee-ter per hour.
The speed in the second case is 9 kilo-mee-ter per hour.
The extra distance covered is 18 kilo-mee-ter.

The formula we need is,

Distance equal to Speed into Time.

Now let us apply this formula.

At 6 kilo-mee-ter per hour,

x equal to 6t

Now look at the second case.

The jogger covers 18 kilo-mee-ter more than the actual distance.

So,

x pluss 18 equal to 9t

Now we already know that x is equal to 6t.

So let us put 6t in place of x.

We get,

6t pluss 18 equal to 9t
so after solving this we got 


T  equal to 6 hours.



Now that we know the time, finding the actual distance is very easy.

Remember, we already have the equation,

x equal to 6t

And we have just found that t is equal to 6.

So let us substitute this value.

x equal to 6 into 6

x equal to 36 kilo-mee-ter

So the actual distance covered by the jogger is "36 kilo-mee-ter".
