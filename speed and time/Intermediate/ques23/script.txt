Now in this problem, the bus speed is given in two different ways.

The speed without stoppage is 90 kilo-mee-ter per hour.
This means, when the bus is actually moving, its speed is 90 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

And the speed with stoppage is 75 kilo-mee-ter per hour.
This means, after counting moving time plus stoppage time, the average speed becomes 75 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

We need to find the "stoppage time" per hour.

Now let us think for one hour.

If the bus does not stop at all, then in 1 hour it can cover 90 kilo-mee-ter.
But because the bus stops in between, in the same 1 hour it covers only 75 kilo-mee-ter.

So because of stoppages, the bus covers less distance.

The lost distance is equal to 90 my-nus 75.

So the lost distance is 15 kilo-mee-ter.

Now be careful here.
This 15 kilo-mee-ter is not actually travelled by the bus.
It is the distance the bus could have travelled, if it had not stopped.

Now we know the formula,

time is equal to distance divided by speed.

So the time spent in stoppages is equal to the time needed to cover this lost distance at the actual moving speed.

In simple words, during the stoppage time, the bus could have covered this 15 kilo-mee-ter distance at 90 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

So here, lost distance is 15 kilo-mee-ter.
And actual moving speed is 90 kilo-mee-ter per hour.

Therefore, stoppage time is equal to 15 by 90 hour.

After simplifying this, we get 1 by 6 hour.

Now 1 by 6 hour means,
1 by 6 into 60 minutes.

So stoppage time is 10 minutes.

Hence, the bus stops for 10 minutes per hour.

So the final answer will be "10 minutes", which is option B.
