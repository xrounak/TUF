Imagine a person walking for the same number of hours in two different cases.

In the first case, the person walks at 15 kilometres per hour.

In the second case, the person walks faster, at 20 kilometres per hour.

Because the person walks faster, the distance also becomes longer.
The extra distance is 30 kilometres.

We need to find the distance covered in the first case.

Let us call this distance D kilometres.

So the first distance is D.

The second distance is 30 kilometres more.
So it becomes D pluss 30.

Now look at the important clue: the time is the same.

Time is equal to distance divided by speed.

For the first case, time is D divided by 15.

For the second case, time is D pluss 30, divided by 20.

Since both times are equal, we can write:

D divided by 15 is equal to D pluss 30, divided by 20.

Now cross-multiply.

20 D is equal to 15 multiplied by D pluss 30.

Opening the bracket gives:

20 D is equal to 15 D pluss 450.

Now subtract 15 D from both sides.

5 D is equal to 450.

Divide both sides by 5.

D is equal to 90.

So the actual distance travelled is 90 kilometres.

The correct answer is option D, 90 kilometres.
